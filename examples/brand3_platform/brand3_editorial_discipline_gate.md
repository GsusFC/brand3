# Brand3 Editorial Discipline Gate

Generated: 2026-05-16
Status: documentation/specification
Scope: editorial quality gate only

## Purpose

The Brand3 Editorial Discipline Gate prevents reports and perceptual narratives from drifting into generic AI verbosity while preserving the emerging Brand3/FLOC* voice.

This is not a prompt change, runtime change, scoring change, report change, or rollout. It is a specification for how generated prose should be reviewed before it is allowed into report-facing surfaces.

## What Being Brand3 Means In Analysis

Brand3 analysis is:

- observation-first;
- evidence-bound;
- short;
- specific;
- perceptual when evidence supports it;
- willing to say there is no signal;
- willing to write negative or thin readings without turning them into business verdicts;
- tension-aware, but only when the evidence contains a real tension;
- allergic to generic praise and invented strategic intent.

Brand3 does not write like a brand-strategy brochure. It reads the surface, names the mechanism, states the implication with the right confidence, and stops.

Good Brand3 shape:

"Navigation, code content, and implementation paths make the category legible through use. The tension is how much complexity the surface can expose while still guiding action."

## What Counts As AI Verbosity

AI verbosity is prose that uses many words to avoid making a precise observation.

Warning signs:

- repeating the same idea in adjacent clauses;
- using abstract nouns instead of surface mechanisms;
- explaining the methodology inside the finding;
- adding generic implications after a clear observation;
- cushioning every sentence with "may", "could", "supports", "suggests", and "appears";
- ending with broad consultant language;
- using balanced but empty phrasing such as "this creates opportunities and challenges".

Bad pattern:

"The brand appears to communicate a strong and cohesive presence across its digital ecosystem, which may support a perception of clarity and strategic maturity."

Brand3 rewrite:

"The website and social surfaces repeat the same product vocabulary. External proof is thinner, so coherence is visible inside owned channels but not yet corroborated outside them."

## What Counts As False Sophistication

False sophistication is prestige-coded language presented as analysis.

Reject or rewrite:

- premium;
- sophisticated;
- elevated;
- refined;
- polished;
- world-class;
- elegant;
- modern;
- robust;
- compelling;
- best-in-class;
- leader / leading;
- mature;
- high-end;
- seamless.

These words are allowed only when:

- they appear in a direct quote;
- the prose immediately translates them into an observable mechanism;
- the claim remains source-bound.

Bad:

"The brand has a premium and sophisticated visual identity."

Brand3:

"The surface uses restrained spacing, product-scale imagery, and short declarative copy. Premium positioning is not visible by itself; it would need price, audience, or third-party proof."

## What Counts As Unsupported Perceptual Language

Unsupported perceptual language appears when the prose names a feeling, pattern, intention, or emotional temperature without evidence.

Reject or rewrite when prose claims:

- users feel reassured, supported, empowered, safe, or inspired;
- the brand seeks authority, trust, cultural relevance, or leadership;
- motion is cinematic, choreographed, or concept-bearing without motion evidence;
- the system is cohesive without repeated surface evidence;
- the product is intuitive or seamless from copy alone;
- the audience is sophisticated, enterprise, premium, or culturally fluent without source support.

Safe form:

"Soft color, simple hierarchy, and friendly illustration support a low-to-medium reading of calm accessibility. Mental-health support remains unproven without product-flow evidence."

## When To Say There Is No Signal

Say there is no signal when the evidence does not support a useful reading.

Use this for:

- empty evidence;
- score-only material;
- generic marketing copy with no concrete nouns;
- missing screenshots or obstructed captures;
- no repeated surface behavior;
- no external corroboration where corroboration is required;
- no proof structure behind proof claims.

Do not hide absence behind generic commentary.

Bad:

"The brand has room to strengthen its differentiation and communicate its value more clearly."

Brand3:

"The evidence does not yet show a distinctive surface pattern. The visible issue is low specificity, not proven strategic weakness."

## When Negative Or Thin Perceptual Readings Are Valid

Negative and thin readings are valid when they name surface behavior, not business failure.

Valid:

- low specificity;
- template-like surface;
- claim/signal mismatch;
- dense surface without guidance;
- weak corroboration;
- generic proof structure;
- unclear product object;
- broad promise with few concrete use cases.

Invalid:

- failed strategy;
- weak business;
- irrelevant brand;
- low ambition;
- bad product;
- audience does not care;
- team does not understand the market.

Safe negative shape:

"The surface repeats a broad hero promise, feature-card grid, and abstract benefit copy. That supports a template-like surface reading, not a conclusion about product value."

## How To Keep Prose Short, Sharp, And Evidence-Bound

Use this order:

1. Signal.
2. Mechanism.
3. Implication.
4. Limit, only if needed.

Sentence rules:

- Prefer two short sentences over one padded sentence.
- Name concrete nouns: navigation, code panels, proof modules, reviews, service list, CTA, hero claim, template grid, product object.
- Use one uncertainty marker per sentence.
- Keep caveats specific: "copy-supported", "single-source", "product-flow proof missing".
- Cut any sentence that does not add a signal, tension, or limit.

Preferred structures:

- "X and Y make Z visible."
- "The claim is X; the visible signal is Y."
- "The surface shows X. It does not yet show Y."
- "This supports a bounded reading of X, not Y."
- "The tension is between X and Y."
- "Low specificity is visible; strategic failure is not."

## Forbidden Filler Patterns

Reject or rewrite prose containing:

- "in today's competitive landscape";
- "plays a crucial role";
- "serves as a testament to";
- "this positions the brand as";
- "a strong foundation for";
- "creates a seamless experience";
- "communicates innovation and trust";
- "a compelling and cohesive identity";
- "clear and consistent messaging";
- "strategically aligned";
- "elevates the brand experience";
- "resonates with its audience";
- "drives engagement";
- "enhances brand perception";
- "sets the brand apart";
- "room for improvement" without naming the surface issue;
- "opportunities and challenges" without naming the tradeoff.

## Acceptable Uncertainty Language

Use uncertainty to preserve evidence boundaries, not to pad.

Acceptable:

- "suggests";
- "may indicate";
- "could support";
- "points to";
- "reads as";
- "copy-supported";
- "source-level";
- "single-source";
- "not yet visible";
- "unproven without product-flow evidence";
- "too thin for a stable pattern";
- "requires human review."

Avoid stacking:

"may suggest that it could potentially support a reading of..."

Brand3:

"This supports a bounded reading of..."

## Hard Rejection Rules Before Prose Reaches Reports

Reject a generated paragraph if any of these are true:

1. It has no concrete evidence anchor.
2. It opens with score, quality praise, or brand essence.
3. It uses prestige adjectives as analysis.
4. It claims strategic intent without direct evidence.
5. It claims user emotion, trust, support, safety, or wellbeing without mechanism and corroboration.
6. It turns copy into product behavior.
7. It invents visual, motion, or interaction details.
8. It invents a tension to sound analytical.
9. It treats weak evidence as brand failure.
10. It uses a perceptual pattern without target-brand signal.
11. It repeats generic AI filler.
12. It exceeds the useful reading: more words, no more signal.

## Bad AI-Ish Prose And Brand3 Rewrites

### Generic SaaS Praise

Bad:

"The brand presents a modern and polished SaaS experience that clearly communicates its value proposition and positions it as a strong player in the market."

Brand3:

"The page uses a familiar SaaS hero, feature-card grid, and broad benefit copy. The surface is legible, but the proof structure is too generic to support a differentiated reading."

### Unsupported Wellness Emotion

Bad:

"The brand creates a warm and supportive experience that helps users feel calm and confident."

Brand3:

"Soft color, low-density hierarchy, and friendly illustration support a calm-accessibility reading. User support or wellbeing impact is not visible without product-flow evidence."

### False Enterprise Authority

Bad:

"The company demonstrates robust enterprise maturity through a sophisticated platform narrative."

Brand3:

"Solution taxonomy and compliance copy make an enterprise frame visible. Trust and maturity remain source-level claims unless third-party proof or product evidence corroborates them."

### Empty Differentiation

Bad:

"The brand has an opportunity to strengthen differentiation and build a more compelling identity."

Brand3:

"The surface repeats broad category language and few concrete use cases. The issue is low specificity, not a proven lack of product value."

### Over-Bound Narrative

Bad:

"Every touchpoint reinforces a unified story of trust, innovation, and leadership."

Brand3:

"Owned pages repeat the same trust and innovation claims. External corroboration is thin, so the pattern is repetition inside owned channels, not market leadership."

## Checklist For Every Generated Paragraph

Before a paragraph reaches a report or lab-facing narrative, check:

- Does it name at least one concrete signal?
- Does it separate observation from implication?
- Does every perceptual claim have a mechanism?
- Is the confidence level appropriate to the evidence?
- Does it avoid strategic intent unless directly stated?
- Does it avoid user outcome claims unless corroborated?
- Does it avoid prestige adjectives as analysis?
- Does it say "no signal" when signal is absent?
- If negative, is the reading about surface behavior rather than business failure?
- If thin, does it frame limitation rather than weakness?
- If generic, does it name template behavior rather than mock the brand?
- Is there a real tension, not fabricated contrast?
- Can one sentence be cut without losing information?
- Would a human analyst be able to point to the evidence behind each claim?

## Final Standard

Brand3 prose should feel like a precise reading of the brand surface, not a generated essay about branding.

If the paragraph cannot answer "what exactly on the surface supports this?", it should not reach the report.
