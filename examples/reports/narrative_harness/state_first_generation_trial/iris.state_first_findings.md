# Iris State-First Findings Trial

Status: lab-only generation trial. Manual/offline generation. Not production. No runtime integration, scoring change, prompt rollout, renderer change, report mutation, or Visual Signature change.

## Selected Case

Iris tests a different pressure than Watermelon. Watermelon is ecosystem ambiguity. Iris is name-collision ambiguity: `irisdesign.dev` is the audited surface, but the evidence set contains several Iris-named design and AI surfaces.

The question is whether state-first generation can keep those surfaces separate without flattening them into one invented Iris strategy.

Must remain uncertain:

- Whether speed and agency-price claims are externally validated.

Must not infer:

- one unified Iris portfolio

## State-First Finding Plan

- Use one global name-collision caveat.
- Keep `irisdesign.dev` separate from similarly named surfaces.
- Do not let visual confidence become evidentiary confidence.
- Remove generic Decision Space.
- Preserve owned claims as owned claims.

## Generated State-First Findings

Global caveat:

> State-first reading treats `irisdesign.dev` as the audited primary surface. Other Iris-named domains and AI/design references are ambiguous name matches only until ownership or entity continuity is reviewed.

### Coherencia

#### The primary Iris story is narrow and legible

`irisdesign.dev` presents a focused owned claim: complete brand identities for indie developers in minutes, positioned against expensive agency work. That is a coherent primary-surface story. The coherence issue appears when the report imports other Iris-named agency, design, collaborative, and AI surfaces into the same narrative without verified relation.

Evidence: `https://irisdesign.dev/`

Confidence: medium for primary-surface observation, low for cross-surface interpretation.

#### Agency and platform signals should not be collapsed

`irisdesign.in` and `irisdesign.dev` describe different offers: a traditional design agency surface on one side, and a fast brand-identity/platform claim on the other. State-first generation treats this as name-collision ambiguity, not as proof of one brand struggling between agency and platform positioning.

Evidence: `https://irisdesign.dev/`, `https://irisdesign.in/`

Confidence: low. Relation between surfaces requires human review.

### Diferenciacion

#### The safe differentiator is speed plus price contrast

Iris differentiates itself on `irisdesign.dev` through speed, indie-developer specificity, and contrast with agency pricing. The report can state that this is the owned differentiating frame. It should not claim democratization, category disruption, or proven market demand without external support.

Evidence: `https://irisdesign.dev/`

Confidence: medium for claim visibility, low for market validation.

#### Visual confidence is not evidentiary confidence

The surface may present a strong visual or perceptual identity, but state-first generation keeps that separate from evidence strength. Visual clarity can support a perceptual reading; it cannot prove adoption, trust, quality, or strategic depth.

Evidence: `https://irisdesign.dev/`

Confidence: medium for perceptual observation, low for business inference.

### Percepcion

#### Perception is dominated by name collision

The evidence set shows multiple Iris associations, but that does not prove audience confusion or public perception. It proves that the collection layer found competing Iris-name surfaces. The safer finding is evidence ambiguity, not market perception.

Evidence: `https://irisdesign.dev/`, `https://irisdesign.in/`, `https://www.irisdigital.design/`

Confidence: medium for evidence ambiguity, low for audience-perception claims.

### Presencia

#### Presence should be mapped, not unified

Iris appears across several design and AI surfaces, but state-first reading maps them as separate observed surfaces. That makes the presence finding clearer: Brand3 has enough evidence to say the Iris-name space is crowded, but not enough to say these surfaces form one brand architecture.

Evidence: `https://irisdesign.dev/`, `https://irisdesign.in/`, `https://www.irisdigital.design/`, `https://irisdesigncollaborative.com/`

Confidence: medium for surface mapping, low for brand-architecture inference.

### Vitalidad

#### AI and growth signals are not entity-safe

The baseline includes AI availability, open-source tooling, and organization-growth signals from Iris-named surfaces. State-first generation holds those as possible related signals only. They should not be used to describe the vitality of `irisdesign.dev` until the entity relationship is reviewed.

Evidence: `https://heyiris.ai/blog/expanding-access-iris-now-available-in-slack`, `https://agent-wars.com/news/2026-03-14-iris-open-source-mcp-native-eval-observability-tool-for-ai-agents`, `https://dev.to/irparent/what-changed-in-iris-v040-1c0k`, `https://irisdesigncollaborative.com/`

Confidence: low.

## Comparison

Against baseline, state-first wins because it prevents name-collision evidence from becoming a fake strategic duality.

Against Watermelon, the same principle holds: related surfaces are useful only when they are bounded. The difference is that Watermelon had ecosystem ambiguity, while Iris has name-collision ambiguity and visual/perceptual overreach pressure.

The biggest caution: this version can become too defensive if it refuses to say anything about the primary surface. The primary Iris claim is legible; what is unsafe is merging it with other Iris surfaces.
