Mode: `strong`

```text
separate audited entity evidence from similarly named surfaces and prevent false aliasing
```

Global caveat:

```text
This lab reading treats irisdesign.dev as the audited surface. Other Iris-like domains are evidence of name-collision pressure only; they are not treated as aliases, owned surfaces, or proof of one shared strategy.
```

Core uncertainty rule:

```text
Visual/perceptual confidence may describe visible surface behavior, but it cannot compensate for weak entity or evidentiary confidence.
```

## State-First Findings

### Coherencia

Baseline titles:

- Brand Identity Creation Speed
- Design Agency Positioning
- Brand Intelligence Platform

State-first candidate:

Iris has a coherent owned promise on the audited surface: irisdesign.dev frames brand identity creation as fast, developer-oriented and positioned against expensive agency work. The current evidence does not safely connect that promise to the separate Iris-like agency, digital-design, AI-agent or collaborative domains. Coherence should therefore be read narrowly: the audited page has a clear pitch, but the broader Iris evidence set is entity-ambiguous and should not be collapsed into one brand system.

Evidence used:

- `https://irisdesign.dev/`

Uncertainty retained:

- Whether `irisdesign.in`, `irisdigital.design`, `heyiris.ai`, `iris-ai.dev`, `byiris.io` or `irisdesigncollaborative.com` are related to `irisdesign.dev` remains unverified.
- The three baseline coherence findings without evidence URLs remain low-confidence.

Overreach avoided:

- No claim of one integrated Iris brand architecture.
- No inference that agency, AI-agent or collaborative surfaces are owned by `irisdesign.dev`.

Confidence: low to medium. Human review required.

### Diferenciacion

Baseline titles:

- Brand Identity Speed and Value
- Distinctive Product Development Focus
- Efficiency and Content Quality

State-first candidate:

The differentiated signal that can be safely attached to irisdesign.dev is the speed/value proposition for indie developers. It is a sharp owned claim, but the baseline evidence does not yet prove that the resulting brand work is distinctive, original or strategically deep. The stronger reading is not that Iris already owns a defensible category position; it is that the audited surface makes a focused promise that still needs external proof or product evidence.

Evidence used:

- `https://irisdesign.dev/`

Uncertainty retained:

- Quality, originality and distinctiveness claims remain owned-positioning signals.
- No third-party corroboration is available in the baseline for the speed/value promise.

Overreach avoided:

- No category-leadership claim.
- No conversion of fast delivery into proven strategic quality.

Confidence: low to medium. Human review required.

### Percepcion

Baseline titles:

- Brand Identity Creation Speed
- Agency vs. Platform Positioning
- Experience and Reach Claims

State-first candidate:

Perceptually, the audited Iris surface reads as a fast brand-identity tool for indie developers, not as a fully evidenced agency network or AI-intelligence ecosystem. The confusing part is external to the audited surface: similarly named Iris domains create pressure around what a viewer might think Iris is. That pressure is real as a discoverability and interpretation risk, but it is not proof of shared ownership or one coordinated market story.

Evidence used:

- `https://irisdesign.dev/`
- `https://irisdesign.in/`
- `https://www.irisdigital.design/`

Evidence boundary:

`irisdesign.in` and `irisdigital.design` are used only as name-collision surfaces that may affect perception; they are not treated as owned aliases.

Uncertainty retained:

- The relationship between the audited surface and the other Iris domains is unknown.
- Perceptual confidence about surface clarity is separate from evidentiary confidence about ownership or market reputation.

Overreach avoided:

- No claim that Iris intentionally maintains a dual platform/agency strategy.
- No treatment of name collision as strategic ambiguity.

Confidence: medium. Human review required.

### Presencia

Baseline titles:

- Multiple Domains for Brand Identity
- Focus on Speed and Strategy

State-first candidate:

Presence is the dimension where the name-collision problem should be made explicit. The audit has one primary surface, irisdesign.dev, plus several Iris-like domains in the evidence set. That creates a visibility problem for interpretation, not a verified multi-domain footprint. A safe finding should separate the audited domain from unresolved adjacent names before discussing reach, channel strategy or brand architecture.

Evidence used:

- `https://irisdesign.dev/`
- `https://irisdesign.in/`
- `https://www.irisdigital.design/`

Evidence boundary:

Multiple domains are observable in the evidence set, but their ownership relation is not established.

Uncertainty retained:

- Domain adjacency does not establish an official footprint.
- The presence signal may reflect search/discovery collision rather than brand strategy.

Overreach avoided:

- No consolidation recommendation as if ownership were proven.
- No framing of the surfaces as deliberate segmentation.

Confidence: medium. Human review required.

## Verdict

The state-first candidate is better, safer and clearer than the baseline for Iris.

The biggest improvement is that it stops collapsing Iris-like surfaces into a single implied entity or strategy.

The biggest remaining risk is that this is manually generated prose from lab artifacts. It is not proof that an automated generator can write this reliably.

What still fails:

- Six baseline findings lack local evidence URLs.
- Ownership or relation among Iris-like domains remains unverified.
- The candidate does not prove automated prose quality.
- The candidate is a lab artifact and must not replace the official report.
